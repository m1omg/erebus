Which one first

If you have not played either: The Five Lessons. It is the same story as Before the Garden with nothing in it that tells you where the seventeen years are going — the title, two scenes and five codex entries were rewritten so the turn arrives when the story means it to. Once you have played it, Before the Garden is the same run with the foreshadowing restored, which reads completely differently on a second pass. After the Garden is a separate story that opens in the world left behind by exactly one of the twenty-two endings — The Seven Months, the one where you stand down and the coalition needs until October to break it. Every other ending leads somewhere else, so After is a sequel to a branch, not to the whole thing. All three keep separate saves.

Plain or illustrated

Same story, same scenes, same endings — the difference is that in the illustrated cut whoever is speaking is standing there. Twenty-one portraits: the eight people, and the nine machines, which are not drawn as people. The four characters who are on screen across the whole seventeen years have two portraits each and age between them. Nobody was redrawn — the backgrounds are identical in both cuts. Both editions share one save, so endings you find in either count in the same gallery.

The plain cut is 2.4 MB and the illustrated one is 5.5 MB. On a slow connection, or if you would rather keep the rooms empty, play the plain one — it is not a lesser version, just a different amount of the screen occupied.

How they work

Choices don't announce themselves

No option says “be merciful” or “be ruthless.” Each is a concrete act, and the consequence arrives as someone's reply rather than a number going up.

The sincerity meter lies

When the machine speaks, its lines carry a readout like SINCERITY 66% ±41. It jitters, the error band is enormous, and it is a model with an interest. Distrust it.

One hidden stat is about you

Complicity, or contamination. Past a threshold the screen shudders and the score detunes — and endings open that you can only reach by having been quietly converted.

Verification is a mechanic

Auditing obsessively gates the best routes, and decides whether the clause at the end of the deletion protocol turns out to have been load-bearing.

Technical

Each game is a single self-contained HTML file — no server, no network, no dependencies, playable offline from file://. All 54 backgrounds are inlined as WebP. The score is synthesised live in Web Audio: detuned oscillator pads through a breathing lowpass, a sub with its own LFO, pink-noise air, and randomly scheduled bell tones on a per-mood scale. Nothing is streamed.
